Golden Pinenut Frangipane Tart

17 July 2026by DebbieLeave a comment
Jump to Recipe Jump to Video

This tart brings together the pastry lessons Debbie learned as an apprentice: a crisp shell, a tender almond filling and fruit prepared so every component keeps its texture.

The shortcrust supports a moist frangipane filled with citrus-soaked sultanas, baby mandarins and glacé cherries. Pine nuts toast across the surface while the filling sets gently underneath.

The result is rich but balanced. Citrus lifts the butter and almonds, the fruit stays juicy, and the pastry remains clean enough to slice for afternoon tea or a celebration table.

Why this tart works

Controlled gluten

Butter coats the flour before liquid is added, limiting gluten development and keeping the pastry short rather than chewy.

Stable emulsion

Eggs bind the butter and almond meal so the frangipane sets evenly without separating or becoming oily.

Hydrated fruit

Pre-soaked sultanas stay plump during baking instead of pulling moisture from the almond filling.

Rest and carry-over heat

Chilling limits shrinkage; cooling completes the set and gives the tart its clean, tender slices.

Golden Pinenut Frangipane Tart

Recipe summary: A crisp shortcrust shell with a tender almond filling, citrus-soaked fruit and toasted pine nuts. The method keeps the pastry clean and the frangipane moist, with practical guidance for reliable results.

1 hrPrep time
1 hr 15Cook time
2 hr 15Total time
Servings: 8 peopleCourse: DessertCuisine: Baking
Ingredients Equipment Method Video Notes

Ingredients

Frangipane filling

60 gSultanas
60 gOrangecello
120 gSoft unsalted butter
100 gIcing sugar
100 gAlmond meal
1Egg
120 gPine nuts

Shortcrust base

500 gPlain flour
250 gCold unsalted butter
325 gCaster sugar
1 + 1Egg and extra yolk
40 mlCold water, approximately

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 175°C. Mix flour and salt, then rub in the cold butter until the mixture resembles breadcrumbs.
  2. Blend in sugar, egg and yolk. Add only enough cold water to bring the dough together, then block and chill for 30 minutes.
  3. Roll to roughly 4mm, line the lightly oiled tart tin without stretching, dock the base and chill again before baking until pale gold.
  4. Soak the sultanas in Orangecello. Prepare the citrus and cherries while the pastry rests.
  5. Mix soft butter, icing sugar and almond meal until smooth. Add the egg without whipping, then fold through the fruit and soaking liquid.
  6. Spread a thin layer of fig jam over the shell, add the filling, cover with pine nuts and press them lightly into the surface.
  7. Bake until golden and just set. Cool completely before removing the tin and dusting with icing sugar.

Equipment

  • 22cm deep fluted tart tin
  • Stand mixer and flat beater
  • Rolling pin
  • Mixing bowls
  • Fork and bowl scraper
  • Citrus zester
  • Palette knife
  • Cooling rack

Recipe notes

Keep the pastry butter cold and the frangipane butter soft. Weigh eggs for consistency, soak the fruit before mixing, and avoid aerating the almond filling like a sponge cake.

For an alcohol-free version, soak the fruit in orange, mandarin or apple juice.

How to make it

1

Build a pastry that will not shrink

Mix gently, shape the dough before chilling, and ease it into the flutes rather than stretching it. A second rest after lining lets the gluten relax and the butter firm.

2

Keep the frangipane tender

Combine the filling until smooth but do not cream it. Too much air makes frangipane rise sharply and collapse as it cools.

3

Finish the surface evenly

Spread the filling level, scatter the pine nuts across the top and press them in lightly so they toast without rolling away when the tart is sliced.

4

Bake for texture, not only colour

Look for lightly toasted pine nuts, set edges and a very slight movement in the centre. Let the tart cool completely so the filling finishes setting.

Inclusive ingredient swaps

Change ingredients by function so the pastry and filling retain their intended structure.

  • Gluten-free:
    Use a balanced flour blend with starch and xanthan gum.
  • Dairy-free:
    Choose a firm baking block with roughly 75–80% fat.
  • Alcohol-free:
    Soak the fruit in orange or mandarin juice.
  • Nut-free direction:
    Use toasted seed meal and pumpkin or sunflower seeds.

Common mistakes

Overworking the pastryStop mixing as soon as the dough comes together to avoid a tough crust.
Skipping the chillCold butter and relaxed gluten are what keep the fluted shell in shape.
Over-aerating the fillingFrangipane should be smooth and stable, not whipped like cake batter.
Slicing while warmAllow the butter and egg structure to finish setting before cutting.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my pastry shrink?

It was probably overworked, stretched into the tin or baked before it had chilled thoroughly.

Why is my frangipane dry?

It was likely overbaked. Remove it when the edges are set and the centre still has a slight wobble.

Can I make the tart ahead?

Yes. Bake it a day ahead, keep it covered, and dust it with icing sugar shortly before serving.

Can I freeze it?

Wrap it well, freeze for up to three months, then thaw overnight in the refrigerator.
